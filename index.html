A window switcher for macOS

Flip to any window.

Lightswitch is a featherweight, Spotlight-style window switcher that finds browser tabs and opens apps too. Press ⌘Space, type a few letters of any window, tab or app, and hit Return. That's it.

Light to use

One shortcut, a few letters, Return. Fuzzy matching favours word starts and runs of letters, so a sloppy few keystrokes still land on the right window.

Light on your Mac

About 900 lines of Swift with zero dependencies. It sits in the menu bar at 0% CPU until you call it, and never touches the network.

Switch to window

Light on the eyes

A native Spotlight-style panel in Liquid Glass on macOS 26 and later, with vibrancy on earlier versions. It follows your light or dark appearance.

Every window, tab and app

Lists the titles of all open windows across your apps, including minimized windows and hidden apps. Browser windows are listed as their tabs, in Chrome, Safari, Brave, Arc, Edge and Vivaldi. Apps that aren't running yet show up below, ready to open.

Back and forth

Your previous window is preselected, so ⌘Space then Return flips between your last two windows, even when one of them is on another desktop or in full screen.

Keyboard first

Tab and Shift-Tab, arrow keys or ⌃N / ⌃P to move. ⌘W closes a window or tab and ⌘Q quits its app, right from the list. Esc clears the query, then closes.

Multi-word search

Separate terms with spaces and every one must match, across the window title and the app name, or a tab's title, address and browser.

Private by design

No accounts, no analytics, no network requests. Window titles and tabs are read locally and never leave your Mac.

Out of the way

A small menu bar icon, no Dock icon, and it starts at login so ⌘Space is always ready.

Install

Up and running in a minute.

Download the app, or build it from source if you prefer. Either way it takes about a minute.

1

Download and drag

Open Lightswitch.dmg and drag Lightswitch into Applications. One download for Apple silicon and Intel Macs.

Download Lightswitch.dmg
2

Confirm the first launch

Lightswitch isn't notarized by Apple yet, so macOS asks you to confirm once. Open it and click Done on the warning. Then go to System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll to Security, click Open Anyway next to Lightswitch and confirm with your password or Touch ID.

3

Follow the welcome window

Lightswitch opens a short setup window that ticks off each step as you go. Click Allow Access… and switch on Lightswitch in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility: macOS only shares window titles through Accessibility. Optionally, click Allow… next to each running browser to search its tabs. Reopen the window any time with Setup… in the menu bar.

4

Hand over ⌘Space, or pick another

Spotlight uses ⌘Space out of the box. Lightswitch opens apps too, so you keep Spotlight's most common use. The welcome window's Open Keyboard Settings takes you there: under Keyboard Shortcuts… → Spotlight, untick "Show Spotlight search" or give it another shortcut, and Lightswitch takes over ⌘Space as soon as it's free. Rather keep Spotlight? Click Use ⌥Space, or Choose Another… and press the combination you want. Change Shortcut… in the menu bar icon changes it later.

Prefer to build it yourself?

You'll need macOS 14 or later and the Xcode Command Line Tools (xcode-select --install). A local build skips the Open Anyway step, then continue from step 3.

git clone https://github.com/aftab404/lightswitch.git
cd lightswitch
./build.sh install

Questions

Good to know.

Why does it need Accessibility access?

It's the only way macOS lets an app read other apps' window titles and bring a specific window to the front. Lightswitch uses it for exactly that and nothing else.

Can it open apps, like Spotlight?

Yes. Type a few letters of an app's name and the ones that aren't running yet appear under Applications, below any matching windows. Tab or the arrow keys move between both, and Return opens the app. Lightswitch looks in /Applications, /System/Applications and ~/Applications, including folders one level down such as Utilities, and notices newly installed apps the next time you open it.

Can it switch browser tabs?

Yes, in Chrome, Safari, Brave, Arc, Microsoft Edge, Vivaldi and Chromium. As soon as the panel opens, each browser window is listed as its tabs, current tab first, so ⌘Space then Return still flips back to the window you came from. A tab matches on its title or its address, so jira finds your Jira board even when its title doesn't say Jira. Return makes that tab current, brings its window to the front and switches to the browser. Lightswitch only reads browsers that are already running and never launches one. Firefox can't be supported: it gives other apps no access to its tabs.

Can I close windows and tabs from it?

Yes. Select a window or tab and press ⌘W to close it, as its close button would, or ⌘Q to quit the app it belongs to (for a tab, that's the browser). The panel stays open with the next row selected, so you can tidy up several in a row. Apps still get to ask about unsaved changes, and ⌘W never quits an app. Closed the wrong tab? ⌘⇧T in the browser reopens the last closed tab or window.

Why does macOS ask to let Lightswitch control my browser?

Browsers only share their tabs through Apple Events, the system behind AppleScript, and macOS asks once per browser before an app may send them: when you click Allow… next to a browser in the welcome window, or the first time you open Lightswitch while a browser you haven't been asked about is running. Click Allow to search that browser's tabs. If you'd rather not, click Don't Allow: its windows are still listed as usual. You can change your mind in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Automation → Lightswitch. Lightswitch is ad-hoc signed, so, as with Accessibility, macOS ties your answer to that exact build and asks again after each update. If you build from source, a "Lightswitch Dev" code signing certificate makes the answer stick across rebuilds.

Can I keep Spotlight?

Yes. Leave Spotlight on ⌘Space and give Lightswitch another shortcut: click Use ⌥Space in the welcome window, or choose Change Shortcut… from its menu bar icon and press the combination you want. Or let Lightswitch have ⌘Space and move Spotlight to a different shortcut in Keyboard Shortcuts → Spotlight.

Does it see windows on other Spaces?

Partly. macOS's Accessibility API only lists the windows on the current Space, but it can reach each app's main window, the one you used last, wherever it is, and Lightswitch keeps hold of the windows you've been in. So the window you came from is preselected even on another desktop or in full screen, and ⌘Space then Return takes you back to it. An app's other windows on other Spaces aren't listed. Apps with no window Lightswitch can reach appear as an app row, and choosing one switches to that app and a Space with its windows.

Why does macOS say it can't verify Lightswitch?

Apple only vouches for apps that are notarized, which needs a paid developer account. Lightswitch isn't notarized yet, so macOS asks you to confirm once with Open Anyway in Privacy & Security. The full source is on GitHub, and building it yourself avoids the prompt entirely.

Why do I have to approve Accessibility again after updating?

macOS ties the permission to the exact build of the app, and each version is a new build. Lightswitch opens its welcome window when it starts without access: click Allow Access… there (or Allow… in the panel, or Grant Accessibility Access… in the menu bar). It clears the stale entry so macOS asks again. If you build from source, a "Lightswitch Dev" code signing certificate in Keychain Access makes the permission stick across rebuilds.
